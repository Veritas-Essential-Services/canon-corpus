# DRAFT: asking to use the Hong translations (not sent)

🔴 **Nothing here has been sent.** Adam reads, edits and sends these himself, or
not at all. Brackets mark the lines only he can fill in.

Who holds what is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md), "Who holds the Hong translations": the
copyright owner of record is **Postscript, Inc.** (Howard Hong assigned his
Kierkegaard copyrights to it in 1994-95); **Princeton University Press**
publishes the volumes and runs the permissions desk; the **Hong Kierkegaard
Library** at St. Olaf is a friend, not an owner.

## Email 1: the request

**To:** permissions@press.princeton.edu
(or the Republication Permission Request form at press.princeton.edu, pasting the body)
**Cc:** HKL@stolaf.edu
**Subject:** Permission request: the Hongs' Kierkegaard translations for a free reading library, until the older translations reach the public domain

Dear Princeton permissions team, and the good people of Postscript, Inc.,

Thank you for taking the time to read this. I'll keep it short!

First, a word of thanks. Howard and Edna Hong gave English readers the whole of
Kierkegaard. Kierkegaard's Writings is a monument, and Princeton kept it in print
for nearly fifty years. I'm grateful for both.

My name is Adam [surname]. I'm building the Armarium, a free online reading
library of public-domain classics. Every book is cited the way scholars cite it,
so a reader can move from a passage to its original language and back. We
already carry Kierkegaard's complete Danish text, from the Royal Danish
Library's open edition of Søren Kierkegaards Skrifter.

Here's my request. The Swenson and Lowrie translations will enter the US public
domain one by one between 2035 and 2051. Until each one does, I'd love to offer
readers the Hong translation of that work beside the Danish. When a Swenson or
Lowrie volume becomes free, we'd switch to it, and the Hong text for that work
would come down. Nothing would stay up longer than the gap it fills.

What that would look like:

- The Hong text shown for reading, paragraph by paragraph, beside the Danish.
- Full credit on every page: translators, Postscript, Inc., Princeton University
  Press, and a link to buy the book.
- No downloads of whole volumes. No resale. The Armarium is non-commercial: no
  ads, no fees, nothing for sale.
- We'd take anything down the day you ask.

[OPTIONAL, strike if you'd rather keep the ask narrow: A few works never had an
earlier English translation at all, such as The Concept of Irony, Prefaces and
The Armed Neutrality. If you'd ever be open to those on the same terms, I'd be
glad to talk. But the gap-bridging request above is the heart of it.]

If Postscript, Inc. is the right party to grant this rather than the Press, I'd
be grateful if you'd point me to them.

I'm happy to send screenshots, a test page, or anything else that would help.
And if the answer is no, I understand completely. Thank you kindly for
considering it!

Warmly,
Adam [surname]

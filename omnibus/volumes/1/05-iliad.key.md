# The Iliad: teacher's key

This file holds short answers to the comprehension questions and notes on
each session's Summa question. Interpretive questions have no single right
answer. Grade those on use of the text.

## Session 1
1. Chryseis is the daughter of Chryses, priest of Apollo. Apollo sends a
   plague until she is returned. Agamemnon takes Briseis from Achilles.
2. Minerva (Athena) catches him by the hair and restrains him (book 1).
3. That Jove give the Trojans the upper hand until the Achaeans honor him.
4. Paris and Menelaus. Venus (Aphrodite) snatches Paris away before
   Menelaus can kill him.

*Summa.* Honor, and specifically public recognition of worth. Briseis is
the token, not the thing. Push students past "he wanted the girl back."

## Session 2
1. Venus and Mars, with Minerva's help.
2. To have the women of Troy pray to Minerva. He also finds Paris and sends
   him back to fight, and he sees Andromache and Astyanax.
3. They discover their grandfathers were guest-friends, refuse to fight,
   and exchange armor. Glaucus gives gold for bronze.
4. He weighs the fates of the two armies. The Achaeans' side sinks.

*Summa.* Watch for the connection to Scripture: fear of the Lord is the
*beginning* of wisdom, and it ends in trust and love (1 John 4:18). Nobody
in the Iliad loves Jove.

## Session 3
1. Tripods, gold, cauldrons, horses, seven women including Briseis
   (untouched), later a daughter in marriage and seven cities. There is no
   apology. Agamemnon even says Achilles should yield to him as the
   kinglier man.
2. Odysseus (Ulysses), Ajax, Phoenix.
3. Stay and die young with undying fame, or go home and live long, unknown
   (`iliad-butler:9.334`).
4. The Trojans break through it (book 12). After the war the gods destroy
   it entirely.

*Summa.* The gifts can't restore what he believes Agamemnon took, which is
the public acknowledgment that Achilles is the best of the Achaeans. His
pride now rejects even what he once demanded.

## Session 4
1. To wear Achilles' armor and lead the Myrmidons. Achilles warns him to
   drive the Trojans from the ships and come back, and not to press on to
   Troy.
2. He presses on toward the walls of Troy. Along the way he kills Sarpedon,
   son of Jove.
3. Apollo, Euphorbus, Hector.
4. Hector strips it from Patroclus and puts it on.

*Summa.* Good answers name more than one agent: Apollo, Euphorbus, Hector,
Patroclus' own disobedience, and Achilles' pride, which sent another man to
do his fighting.

## Session 5
1. She rises from the sea with her Nereids and comes to comfort him,
   foretelling that his death will follow Hector's.
2. Any four: earth, sky and sea with the stars; a city at peace with a
   wedding and a lawsuit; a city at war under siege; plowing; a harvest; a
   vineyard; cattle attacked by lions; sheep; a dancing floor; the river
   Oceanus around the rim.
3. Agamemnon returns Briseis with the gifts and swears he never touched
   her. Achilles renounces his anger (book 19).
4. Achilles has choked it with Trojan dead and ignored its plea to stop.

*Summa.* That the world is made for peace, marriage, justice, labor and
feasting, and war is the exception at the edge. Achilles is carrying the
whole ordered world on his arm while he unmakes it.

## Session 6
1. Shame. He ignored Polydamas' advice to retreat, and his rashness cost
   Trojan lives. He would rather die than face the townspeople.
2. She takes the form of his brother Deiphobus, so Hector believes he has
   help. Then she vanishes.
3. He pierces the tendons of the feet, drags it behind his chariot, and
   leaves it to lie. Apollo protects it from decay and harm. The gods send
   Thetis to tell Achilles to give it up.
4. Hector's body, for ransom. Achilles gives it, washed and anointed, and
   grants a truce for the funeral.

*Summa.* The body of his son, and a moment of shared humanity with his
enemy. But he doesn't win peace, and he doesn't win Troy.

## Evaluation
1. Agamemnon and Achilles. Agamemnon took Briseis.
2. Achilles' dearest friend. He is killed by Apollo, Euphorbus and Hector
   while fighting in Achilles' armor.
3. Short life with deathless fame, or long life without it.
4. Look for: Priam comes by night, kisses Achilles' hands, appeals to him
   through his father, both weep, and the body is returned.
5. Accept Butler's wording (`iliad-butler:6.102`).

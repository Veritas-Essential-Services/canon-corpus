# Rights

This follows canon-corpus rule 6 and the 2026-07-26 rights gate.

- **US public domain as of 2026:** anything published in 1930 or earlier.
  Each year on 1 January, one more year enters (publication year + 96).
- **Translations carry their own copyright.** Homer is PD; a 1990
  translation of Homer isn't. The `edition` column in `plan/omnibus.csv`
  names the exact translation, and that's the one whose rights line gets
  read.
- **`pd-us`** means PD in the US but still under copyright in life+70
  countries such as the UK (Gilbert Murray, T. S. Eliot). Fine to print and
  sell in the US. Check before selling abroad.
- **`not-pd`** rows are in the plan only so the gap stays visible (Lewis,
  Tolkien, Orwell, Huxley). Chapters on them may cite, summarize and map
  facts. They never reproduce the text. The gap report prints the date each
  one enters US PD.
- **CCEL** asks for non-commercial use of some of its prepared editions.
  The underlying text is PD, but check each work before selling a printed
  anthology built from CCEL markup.
- **Before a title is marked `pd`:** read the rights line of the exact
  edition (for Gutenberg, grep the header for `COPYRIGHTED Project
  Gutenberg`), then record the translator.
- **The editorial layer** (essays, questions, introductions) is original
  work and belongs to the author. Decide its license before publishing.
